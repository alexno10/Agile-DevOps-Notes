# 🎭 Scrum Roles (Accountabilities)

> [!NOTE]  
> The Scrum Team consists of one Scrum Master, one Product Owner, and Developers. Within a Scrum Team, there are no sub-teams or hierarchies. It is a cohesive unit of professionals focused on one objective at a time, the Product Goal.

Scrum Teams are cross-functional, meaning the members have all the skills necessary to create value each Sprint. They are also self-managing, meaning they internally decide who does what, when, and how.

---

## 👑 The Product Owner (PO)

The Product Owner is accountable for maximizing the value of the product resulting from the work of the Scrum Team. How this is done may vary widely across organizations, Scrum Teams, and individuals.

### Key Responsibilities
- **Product Vision:** Developing and explicitly communicating the Product Goal.
- **Backlog Management:** Creating, ordering, and clearly communicating Product Backlog items.
- **Stakeholder Management:** Acting as the bridge between the development team and the stakeholders. Gathering requirements, managing expectations, and conveying feedback.
- **Decision Making:** Deciding when to release increments and what features to prioritize based on ROI and business value.

> [!WARNING]  
> **Common Mistakes:**
> - Being an "absent" Product Owner who is not available to answer the team's questions.
> - Acting as a dictator rather than a collaborator.
> - Allowing the Product Backlog to become a dumping ground for every possible idea, rather than a curated list of valuable work.

---

## 🛡️ The Scrum Master (SM)

The Scrum Master is accountable for establishing Scrum as defined in the Scrum Guide. They do this by helping everyone understand Scrum theory and practice, both within the Scrum Team and the organization. The Scrum Master is a **servant leader**.

### Key Responsibilities
- **Coaching:** Coaching the team members in self-management and cross-functionality.
- **Impediment Removal:** Causing the removal of impediments to the Scrum Team’s progress.
- **Facilitation:** Ensuring that all Scrum events take place and are positive, productive, and kept within the timebox.
- **Organizational Change:** Helping employees and stakeholders understand and enact an empirical approach for complex work.

> [!TIP]  
> **Anti-patterns to Avoid:**
> - Acting as a project manager (assigning tasks, micro-managing).
> - Solving all problems *for* the team instead of teaching the team how to solve their own problems.
> - Focusing only on the team and ignoring the broader organizational impediments.

---

## 💻 The Developers (Development Team)

Developers are the people in the Scrum Team that are committed to creating any aspect of a usable Increment each Sprint.

### Key Characteristics
- **Self-organizing:** They decide how to turn Product Backlog items into working increments of software.
- **Cross-functional:** As a team, they have all the skills needed to deliver the increment.
- **Accountability:** They hold each other accountable as professionals.
- **Size:** Typically 3 to 9 members. Small enough to remain nimble and large enough to complete significant work within a Sprint.

### Key Responsibilities
- Creating a plan for the Sprint (the Sprint Backlog).
- Instilling quality by adhering to a Definition of Done.
- Adapting their plan each day toward the Sprint Goal.
