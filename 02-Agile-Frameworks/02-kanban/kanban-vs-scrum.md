# ⚖️ Kanban vs. Scrum

While both Scrum and Kanban are popular Agile frameworks, they operate on very different paradigms. Scrum is prescriptive and iterative, whereas Kanban is continuous and flow-based.

## 📊 Comparison Table

| Feature | 🏉 Scrum | 📋 Kanban |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Roles** | Required (Scrum Master, Product Owner, Developers) | No required roles (respects current titles) |
| **Cadence / Iterations** | Fixed timeboxes (Sprints, usually 2 weeks) | Continuous flow (no required timeboxes) |
| **Planning** | At the beginning of each Sprint | Just-in-time planning / continuous grooming |
| **Changes** | Discouraged during a Sprint | Can be added anytime if WIP limits allow |
| **Key Metrics** | Velocity, Burndown charts | Cycle Time, Lead Time, Throughput, CFD |
| **Work Limits** | Limited by Sprint capacity | Limited by WIP (Work In Progress) limits |
| **Estimations** | Essential (Story Points) | Optional (focus on breaking work down) |
| **Best For** | Predictable scopes, product development teams | Operations, support, continuous delivery |

---

## 🤔 When to Choose Which?

### Choose **Scrum** When:
* You are building a new product from scratch.
* The team needs a rigid structure to build Agile habits.
* You need to align multiple teams on a predictable, synchronized delivery schedule.
* Stakeholders require regular, scheduled demos of new increments.

### Choose **Kanban** When:
* Work is unpredictable or reactive (e.g., DevOps, SRE, IT Support).
* The team is experienced with Agile and finds Scrum's overhead (meetings, estimations) wasteful.
* You have a continuous delivery pipeline (CI/CD) and want to release features as soon as they are ready.
* Priorities shift daily or weekly.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> A common anti-pattern is teams failing at Scrum and switching to "Kanban" simply by removing rules. Real Kanban requires strict discipline regarding WIP limits and explicit policies.

---

## 🤝 Scrumban: The Best of Both Worlds?

**Scrumban** is a hybrid methodology that combines the structure of Scrum with the flexibility of Kanban.

* **From Scrum:** Daily stand-ups, retrospectives, and defined roles (like Product Owner).
* **From Kanban:** WIP limits, continuous flow, and pull-based systems instead of batch Sprint planning.

> [!TIP]
> Scrumban is an excellent transition framework for teams looking to move from Scrum to Kanban without shocking the system.

## 👍👎 Pros and Cons

### Scrum
**Pros:**
* Highly predictable rhythm.
* Clear expectations and defined roles.
* Built-in opportunities for team reflection and pivoting.

**Cons:**
* High meeting overhead.
* Rigid rules can frustrate experienced teams.
* Artificial batching of work (Sprints) can delay releases.

### Kanban
**Pros:**
* Highly flexible to changing priorities.
* Lower meeting overhead.
* Optimizes directly for fast delivery and flow.

**Cons:**
* Easy to misuse (often becomes just a "to-do list").
* Lack of timeboxes can lead to endless development cycles for complex features.
* Harder to forecast long-term product roadmaps.
